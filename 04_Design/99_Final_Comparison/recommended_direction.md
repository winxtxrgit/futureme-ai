# Recommended Direction

## Best overall concept — Aurora

Aurora is the best overall direction because it packages the previously recommended hybrid into one coherent product: a trustworthy coach flow, short evidence missions, three explainable route strategies, and an editable future-self roadmap. Its modern Gen-Z visual language makes the experience presentation-ready without introducing a public feed, ranking, likes, or deterministic prediction.

## Best for a Hackathon presentation — Aurora

Aurora has the strongest end-to-end stage story: a student answers one real-experience prompt, corrects a live evidence item, completes a short mission, reveals three routes, and adds one reversible step to a glowing roadmap. QuestMap remains the strongest focused mission concept when the demo must be narrower.

## Easiest to build — Clarity

Clarity uses standard forms, tables, cards, and a linear roadmap. It has the fewest custom visualization requirements, the strongest accessibility starting point, and the lowest content-animation burden.

## Most innovative — Skill Constellation

Skill Constellation reframes careers as connected skill neighborhoods rather than a list of fixed jobs. It is a strong long-term expression of transferable skills, hybrid careers, and DAG relationships, but it is not the right first MVP surface.

## Most scalable — PathLab

PathLab best exposes and operationalizes the underlying evidence, sources, rules, constraints, and recommendation versions. Its modular workspaces can grow into counselor, school, program, scholarship, and employer tools.

## Most suitable for Thai students — Aurora

The source material emphasizes limited counselor capacity, fragmented Thai pathway information, and the need for active trials. Aurora communicates in plain Thai, starts without an account, makes AI inferences editable, and presents academic, vocational, DVE, and skill-building paths without prestige ranking. Its mobile-first energy fits students while consent-scoped summaries preserve trust with teachers and parents.

## Final hybrid recommendation

Build **Future Me — Aurora** with:

1. **Aurora shell** — dark-first/light-capable, Thai-first landing, onboarding, interview, results, dashboard, and roadmap.
2. **Compass Coach conversation model** — low-pressure prompts, reflection, correction, pause, and human handoff.
3. **QuestMap mission module** — short scenario trials that create evidence, with no points, rankings, or streak pressure.
4. **PathLab evidence drawer** — source, status, uncertainty, freshness, edit, and delete for every inference.
5. **Timefold future-self and roadmap** — conditional scenario plus editable branches and a complete list fallback.
6. **Clarity safeguards** — WCAG 2.2 AA target, semantic forms, restrained result language, and consent-scoped print/share summaries.

## Why this combination

- **User needs:** supports “I don’t know,” real behavior, multiple routes, and a human conversation.
- **Project objectives:** visibly differentiates from static tests while preserving the documented architecture.
- **Resources/time:** core experience can be prototyped without building a full graph renderer or story CMS.
- **Technical feasibility:** rule engine + RAG + structured LLM output maps cleanly to the UI.
- **Presentation impact:** the original Aurora illustration, evidence drawer, three-route reveal, and future roadmap create one coherent memorable demo.
- **Future expansion:** evidence/source objects and DAG nodes scale to more programs, schools, internships, and languages.

## Delivery caveat

Aurora has medium–high implementation effort. If the team cannot complete both themes and visual QA, keep the Aurora tokens and hero but ship the Compass Coach/Clarity component set first. Recommendation quality, source freshness, consent, safety, and accessibility must take priority over mesh effects or motion.
